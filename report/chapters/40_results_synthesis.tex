\subsection{The one-paragraph version}

On a real 195-name, 16.7-year total-return panel, an adaptively estimated basket spread
does what statistical arbitrage theory says it should: it is stationary when the hedge is
rolled forward (ADF $p<10^{-3}$) and non-stationary when the hedge is fixed for sixteen
years ($p=0.083$); it reverts with a half-life of $\approx9.4$ bars; the strategy holds
$\approx1.8$ half-lives and captures $\approx70\%$ of the reversion; its standardized form
predicts the direction of convergence out of sample with IC $0.13$--$0.21$; shrinkage and
recursive smoothing improve the \emph{net} result by cutting turnover rather than by
finding more signal; and flexible nonlinear models lose to the regularized linear map
because the sample is too small for their variance. All of that is real, reproducible, and
mechanistically explained. And all of it is worth, net of 10\,bps per side, a Sharpe of
$0.05$--$0.11$ on the best single basket and $-0.40$ on the four-basket book --- numbers
whose own standard error is five times their size, that fail an uncorrected 5\% test at the
best of 44 tries ($p=0.059$), that survive no data-snooping correction
(Bonferroni 0 survivors; RC $p=0.868$; SPA $p=0.638$; DSR $0.305$), and that would require
23--78 years (best of 44) or 366--1{,}259 years (best \code{ADBE} variant) of daily data to
confirm. The structure is a finding; the magnitude is not.

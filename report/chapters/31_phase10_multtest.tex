\subsection{Goal}

Answer one question with a number: \emph{after correcting for everything we searched over,
is the best net Sharpe distinguishable from zero?} This phase is the adversarial
self-critique of the previous six, and its answer is the headline result of the project.

\subsection{The hypothesis count, assembled honestly}

The audit universe is the \emph{symmetric product} of the four baskets and the eleven
hedge configurations that were actually traded and reported across Phases 4--9:

Using the same symmetric grid for every basket is deliberate: it makes the data-snooping
universe a clean product set, so $M$ is not an argument about which configurations
``count''. All 44 are simulated on the identical causal pipeline at 10\,bps one-way and
aligned on a \emph{common} daily calendar (2010-11-03 to 2026-09-01, $T=3{,}979$ bars),
so the Reality Check and SPA operate on a genuine $(T,M)$ matrix rather than on ragged
series. The aligned matrix is persisted as \code{results/tables/multtest\_pnl.csv}, which
is what makes the power and deflated-Sharpe calculations of
Section~\ref{sec:mt:power}--\ref{sec:mt:dsr} reproducible without re-simulating.

\begin{remark}[Honesty: what is \emph{not} in $M$]
$M=44$ counts traded strategy configurations. It does \emph{not} count: the four baskets
themselves (chosen a priori by sub-industry, but still chosen); the $z$-bands $2.0/0.5$;
the 10\,bps cost level; the 2010--2026 window; or the $16+16$ cells of the
Phase~\ref{ch:phase7} sensitivity heatmaps, whose best cell was reported. A fully
accounted audit would push $M$ into the hundreds, which would make the Bonferroni
threshold correspondingly harsher and the conclusion stronger. $M=44$ is therefore a
\emph{lower bound} on the search breadth, and the verdict below is conservative in the
direction that matters --- it under-corrects and still finds nothing.
\end{remark}

\caption{Diebold--Mariano forest on \code{ADBE}: mean daily net-P\&L difference of the

\label{fig:multtest_forest}

The reference is the best \code{ADBE} hedge (Kalman $q=10^{-5}$). Mean daily differences
range from $+0.03$ to $+0.86$\,bps with HAC standard errors of $0.088$--$1.163$\,bps,
giving DM statistics between $0.04$ and $1.23$ and $p$-values between $0.220$ and $0.965$.
Every 95\% interval spans zero.

The economically largest and statistically weakest pair is the one that matters most:
Kalman $q{=}10^{-5}$ versus Kalman $q{=}10^{-3}$ differs by $0.864$\,bps/day
($\approx2.2\%$/year on \$1 gross) with a standard error of $0.705$\,bps, $\mathrm{DM}=1.23$,
$p=0.22$. A $2\%$/year improvement in a market-neutral book is a real edge; sixteen years
of one daily basket cannot demonstrate it. The same is true of the smooth-versus-churny
comparison that Phases 5 and 8 emphasised: the direction is consistent and mechanistically
explained, and it is inside the noise.

\subsection{Interpretation}

\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{A\arabic*.}]
  \item \textbf{The answer to the phase's question is ``no''.} Not ``probably not'' --- no.
    The best raw $p$ in a 44-strategy search is $0.059$, which fails even the uncorrected
    5\% test. There is no version of the multiple-testing methodology under which this set
    yields a discovery, because there is no single-strategy discovery to correct.
  \item \textbf{The bootstrap tests agree with the per-strategy tests, and that agreement
    is informative.} White's RC ($p=0.868$) and Hansen's SPA ($p=0.638$) are much less
    conservative than Bonferroni and account for the cross-strategy dependence explicitly;
    they still find nothing. When a conservative test and two powerful tests agree, the
    conclusion is robust to methodology.
  \item \textbf{The deflated Sharpe ratio adds the selection effect.}
    $\mathrm{SR}_0=0.474$ is what you would expect the best of 44 trials to look like if
    all 44 were noise, given the observed cross-trial dispersion of Sharpers. Our best is
    $0.346$ --- below the null benchmark --- giving $\mathrm{DSR}=0.305$ against a $>0.95$
    bar. The fat tails (excess kurtosis $13.4$) make this worse, not better.
  \item \textbf{The power calculation is the deepest result.} Even taking the best observed
    Sharpe at face value and asking only ``how long until we could confirm it?'', the
    answer is $22.6$ years uncorrected and $77.8$ years corrected --- versus the 15.8 years
    we have. For the \code{ADBE} basket the answers are $366$ and $1{,}259$ years. This
    means the negative verdict is not a verdict on this strategy so much as on the
    \emph{experimental design}: single-basket daily-frequency relative value is
    unfalsifiable at any sample size that exists. The design must change
    (Chapter~\ref{ch:conclusion}): more breadth, higher frequency, or lower cost.
  \item \textbf{What the audit does \emph{not} say.} It does not say the spread is not
    stationary (Phase~\ref{ch:phase6}: ADF $p<10^{-3}$, half-life $9.4$ bars --- those are
    properties of a series, not of a searched maximum, and they are unaffected by
    multiplicity). It does not say the hedge is not cointegrated (Johansen rank 2, HAC
    $t=7.3$ and $9.4$ on two of three weights). It does not say the signal has no
    directional content (OOS IC $0.125$--$0.211$ on a \emph{pre-specified} test period,
    positive for every estimator). It says: \emph{the traded, net-of-cost Sharpe of the
    best configuration found in this search is not distinguishable from zero, and could not
    be distinguished from zero with any amount of data at this effect size.}
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{The honesty gate}

\code{environment/check.sh} contains a check that \emph{fails the build} unless
\code{n\_survive\_bonferroni == 0} \emph{and} \code{white\_rc\_p >= 0.05} \emph{and}
\code{hansen\_spa\_p >= 0.05}. This is unusual and deliberate: the project's central
empirical finding is encoded as a regression test, so that a future edit which quietly
produces a ``significant'' result cannot pass the exit gate without an explicit, visible
change to the gate itself. Current status: passes (0 survivors, $p_{\text{RC}}=0.868$,
$p_{\text{SPA}}=0.638$).

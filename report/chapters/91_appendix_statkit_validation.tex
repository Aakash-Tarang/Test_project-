\subsection{What the test design deliberately avoids}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{No recorded-value tests.} Nothing asserts ``the ADF statistic equals
    $-3.412$''. Such a test breaks on a \code{statsmodels} upgrade and tells you nothing
    about correctness.
  \item \textbf{Both size and power, for every test statistic.} A test that rejects
    everything passes a power check and fails a size check; a test that rejects nothing
    passes size and fails power. Several implementations in the literature pass only one.
    Here every statistic has both (ADF, KPSS, DW, LB, BP, White, DM, Bonferroni, Reality
    Check).
  \item \textbf{Degenerate-input tests.} $\theta\to0$ (infinite half-life), short series
    (raise rather than return garbage), singular $X^{\top}X$ (ridge restores invertibility),
    zero-variance series (no division by zero). These are the cases that silently produce
    \code{inf}/\code{nan} in a pipeline and then poison a table.
  \item \textbf{Fixed seeds.} Every bootstrap and simulation test seeds its generator, so a
    failure is reproducible bit-for-bit.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Known limitations of the toolkit}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{ADF/KPSS critical values come from \code{statsmodels}.} They are the
    published/simulated tables, which is better than anything we would derive, but it means
    the toolkit inherits that dependency's conventions (KPSS \code{nlags="auto"} uses the
    Hobijn et al.\ bandwidth; ADF \code{autolag="AIC"}). Both are documented in the wrapper
    signatures.
  \item \textbf{Bootstrap coverage checks are themselves noisy.} With a few hundred
    replications the empirical coverage of a 95\% interval has a standard error of
    $\approx1$--$2\%$, so ``coverage is nominal'' is a weak statement. This is why
    Part~II quotes bootstrap intervals as indicative.
  \item \textbf{Johansen is called with \code{det\_order=0}, \code{k\_ar\_diff=1}.} A VAR
    lag order chosen by information criterion rather than fixed at 1 would be more careful;
    with $m=4$ series and 4{,}070 observations the rank conclusion ($r=2$) is robust to lag
    order in the range 1--4, but that robustness was checked informally, not as a unit test.
    Listed as a small future item.
  \item \textbf{No structural-break test.} Given the 2022 regime shift documented in
    Phase~\ref{ch:phase7}, a Bai--Perron or CUSUM test on the hedge coefficients would be
    informative; its absence is one reason the KPSS/ADF verdicts disagree on the rolling
    spread (Section~\ref{sec:ts:kpss}).
\end{enumerate}
